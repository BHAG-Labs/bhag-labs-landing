Concept C — Stacked Disc (v1, superseded)
Two discs (charcoal + terracotta) on a thin stem. Original direction.
Concept C · v2 — Vesica Piscis (explored, too symmetric)
Two equal circles, d(O₁,O₂) = r. Euclid 1.1. Dropped because the user wanted asymmetry and a leaning stem.
Concept C · v3 — Common External Tangent (offset discs, superseded by v4)
Two unequal circles (r = 30, R = 42), centres offset on x. The stem is the line tangent to both on the same side — and its lean is forced by the size difference:

sin θ = (R − r) / d

Equal discs → perpendicular stem. Unequal ambitions → the stem has no choice but to lean. That's the BHAG premise in one equation: a big hairy audacious goal is always an unequal pair (rigor vs. ambition), and the discipline that spans them is never perpendicular — it always leans.

Reads first as three geometric shapes with a diagonal bar. After a beat: an italic B whose bowls got their sizes from two different hypotheses. The stem "leans on" both discs in the literal tangency sense.

Concept C · v4 — Kissing Circles (superseded by v6)
Discs stacked on a single vertical axis, externally tangent — no gap, they kiss at a single point. In that configuration the centre distance is the sum of radii, so the tangent-line formula simplifies to its cleanest form:

d = R + r ⇒ sin θ = (R − r) / (R + r)

For r = 30, R = 46: θ ≈ 12°. The stem is thicker (stroke 16), longer — it stretches from the crown of the small disc all the way to the base of the large one. Both its length and its angle are computed, not eyeballed.

Narrative: when two unequal ambitions kiss perfectly, the discipline that spans them still has to lean — and the lean is a pure function of their size ratio. Equal ambitions would give a perpendicular stem. BHAGs never do.

Concept C · v6 — Overlapping Discs, Embedded Chord (superseded by v7)
Two unequal discs (r = 30, R = 44) — top one smaller, slightly off-axis. They overlap rather than just kiss, so they intersect at two points and produce a lens region in between, coloured in a third hue (forest #3d5a4a) to mark where rigor meets ambition.

d(O₁, O₂) < R + r ⇒ discs intersect at P₁, P₂

The spine is a tilted chord (not a tangent — a strict tangent line cannot stay flush with both discs beyond its tangent points). It's positioned so its right edge is flush with each disc's left arc, runs from above the small disc down to where the bottom disc's lower-left arc curves back toward its base, and ends with a butt cap — no protrusion, no cream sliver below.

The lens (overlap) sits between the two discs and almost touches the spine, leaving a small curved cream wedge on the left — the little triangle negative space. That triangle is the only place where the two ambitions and the discipline that spans them all visibly separate; everywhere else they merge into a single figure.

Concept C · v7 — Overlapping Discs, Tangent Spine (superseded by v8)
Same disc geometry as v6 (r = 30, R = 44, overlapping, off-axis tilt). Two corrections from v6 feedback:

1. Lens fixed. The overlap is now drawn via SVG clipPath — the bottom disc redrawn in forest green, clipped to the interior of the top disc. This produces the exact intersection with no missing pixels. (v6's hand-traced lens path picked the wrong arcs on the bottom disc and only covered part of the overlap.)

2. Stem returned to a strict tangent. v6's embedded chord was overlapping the discs, which read as "stem cutting through" rather than "stem leaning on". v7 reverts to the common external tangent on the left — it kisses each disc at exactly one point (T₁ and T₂) and stays outside them everywhere else. Round caps, stroke 12 (thinner than v4/v6), extends past both tangent points so it visibly rests against the disc cluster.

d < R + r (overlap) sin θ = (R − r) / d (tangent lean)

The small curved-triangle cream wedge between the spine and the disc cluster is preserved — that's the negative space that makes the figure read as a B rather than a blob.

Concept C · v8 — Refined Proportions, Stronger Lean (superseded by v9)
Three structural refinements from v7:

1. Top disc r: 30 → 28. Upper/lower bowl ratio drops from 68% to 64%, closer to classical B typography where the upper bowl is 60–65% of the lower. The B reads more naturally as a letterform.

2. Spine stroke: 12 → 10. More refined weight. The cream wedge between spine and disc arcs opens slightly, improving B counter readability. Still legible at 16px.

3. Lean: ~2° → ~4.3° from vertical. Direct consequence of the smaller top disc — sin θ = (R − r) / d = 16/55.3 yields a steeper tangent angle. The italic lean is now perceptible without being cartoonish.

r=28 R=44 d≈55.3 → lean ≈ 4.3° · bowl ratio ≈ 0.64

Same overlap + clipPath lens as v7. Same narrative — the lean is forced by the inequality of the discs, not designed.

Concept C · v9 — Tilted B, Separate Stem (superseded by v10)
Returns to v1's structural approach — the stem is a separate bar to the left, the two discs are bowls to the right — but tilted, overlapping, and using the canonical palette.

1. Equal gaps. The stem has ~5px clearance from both the top disc and the bottom disc. No touching. Reads as a proper B spine.

2. Rightward lean & overlap. Top disc (r=32) is 2px right of bottom disc (R=40). ~20px overlap, lens in forest #2a4942. Stem leans ~11° right.

3. Stroke 10. Thicker than v9a's stroke 8, clearly visible as the B's spine even at small sizes. Drawn behind the discs.

r=32 R=40 overlap≈20 lean≈11° stroke 10 gap≈5px

Concept C · v10 — Equal-gap, Butt-cap Spine (superseded by v11)
Scale. r=48, R=60 — fills ~60×80% of the tile. No longer feels small.

5:4 bowl ratio. R/r = 60/48 = 5/4. Upper bowl is exactly four-fifths of the lower. Close to classical B typography and a clean mathematical ratio.

Butt caps. Flat stem ends, perpendicular to the line direction. The angled cut at top and bottom makes the 10.6° lean visible even at small sizes. Architectural, not soft.

Equal gaps. ~6px clearance from both discs. Stroke 14.

Two modes. Light: forest #2a4942 lens. Dark: ochre #c89b27 lens (per brand guide: ochre for highlights on dark).

Lockup. Playfair Display 700 wordmark + Cormorant Garamond italic tagline.

Concept C · v11 — Tangent to Both Discs (superseded by v12)
v10 had two equal gaps. v11 closes both: a single straight stem is tangent to both discs simultaneously, exactly the way the larger disc's leftmost extent reaches further out than the smaller disc's so the stem has two contact points instead of zero.

1. Stem bottom fixed in x. The bottom of the stem stays at (59, 230). Only the angle is free.

2. Disc-pair rotated ~2.91° CW around the bottom disc's centre. Top disc shifts from (147, 84) to (151, 84). Bottom disc unchanged. This is the unique rotation that makes a stem anchored at (59, 230) tangent to both circles at once.

3. Stem angle θ ≈ 13.67° from vertical (up from v10's 10.6°). Computed from the bottom-disc tangency equation:

85 cos θ − 66 sin θ = R + 7 = 67 ⇒ θ ≈ 13.67°

The top-disc tangency then determines the disc-pair rotation. Both contact points T1 (top, ~(103, 72)) and T2 (bottom, ~(86, 150)) lie on the common external tangent of the rotated pair.

4. Round caps, noticeably. Stroke 14 with stroke-linecap="round" — semi-circular ends of radius 7 at both stem termini. Softer than v10's butt caps; the stem reads as drawn, not cut.

Narrative: the spine doesn't float free or cut through — it actually leans on both bowls, with the bigger one catching it further down and the smaller one stopping it higher up. The lean is forced by the geometry: any other stem angle would either gap one disc or pierce it.

Concept C · v12 — Top Tangent, Bottom Sliver (superseded by v13)
v11 kissed both bowls. v12 keeps the top kiss but breathes a small sliver of cream past the lower bowl — the stem leans on the top disc only and just clears the bottom.

1. Top disc tangent (T1). Stem center-line sits at distance r + 7 = 55 from (151, 84). Contact point at ~(98, 69).

2. Bottom disc gap, g ≈ 4 px. Stem center-line distance to (144, 164) is R + 7 + g = 71. The cream sliver between the stem and the leftmost edge of the bottom bowl is the “little triangle” that gives the figure breathing room.

3. Stem angle θ ≈ 16.5° from vertical. Derived from subtracting the two perpendicular-distance equations of a common quasi-tangent:

80 sin θ − 7 cos θ = 12 + g ⇒ θ ≈ 16.5° for g = 4

(Setting g = 0 recovers v11's dual tangency at θ ≈ 13.67°.)

4. Cap on the baseline (option A). Stem center bottom at (54, 217); the round cap of radius 7 lands its lowest point at (52, 224) — exactly the y of the bottom disc's bottom-most point. Spine and bowl share one baseline like a classical typographic B.

5. Stem top. (111, 26) — peeks above the upper bowl by ~17 px. Same projection as v11.

6. Round caps, stroke 14. Both stem termini are fully visible semi-circles. Soft, deliberate, not cut.

Narrative: the stem rests on the upper bowl and is held off the lower one by a hair of cream. The lower bowl's larger radius lets it lean further left without quite reaching the spine. The B reads cleanly at every size because everything terminates on one baseline.

Concept C · v13 — One Shape, One Magic FINAL
v12 had stem and top disc just touching. v13 pushes the stem ~5 px into the top disc and paints both in the same charcoal. They stop reading as two elements and start reading as one shape.

That one shape is the founder + the launchpad. The bottom disc is a different shape entirely, the market, looking for a fit. Where the two shapes overlap is the lens, painted forest in light and ochre on dark. That is the magic. Product-market fit, made literal.

1. Overlap o = 5 px. Stem center-line distance to top disc center: r + 7 − o = 50. About 36% of the stem's width crosses the disc edge. Enough to merge, not enough to lose the stem's line.

2. Gap g = 4 px to bottom disc. Stem center-line distance to (144, 164): R + 7 + g = 71. Cream sliver preserved.

3. Stem angle θ ≈ 20.2° from vertical. The classical formula:

80 sin θ − 7 cos θ = (R + 7 + g) − (r + 7 − o) = 21 ⇒ θ ≈ 20.2°

Each previous version was a different point on this same curve: v10 had two equal gaps, v11 had two tangencies (g = 0, o = 0), v12 had one tangency + one gap (o = 0, g = 4), v13 has overlap + gap. The family is parameterised by o − g.

4. Cap on the baseline. Stem center bottom (49, 217); round cap of radius 7 lands at (47, 224), exactly the bottom disc's lowest y.

5. Same charcoal, same colour, same shape. #1a1a1a for both stem and top disc. #c1502e for the bottom disc. #2a4942 (forest) or #c89b27 (ochre, dark mode) for the lens.
